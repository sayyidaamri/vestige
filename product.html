All measured
elements
explained

ElementTypeWhat it is & where it's found
Magnesium (Mg)EssentialNuts, seeds and leafy greens. Found in nuts, seeds and leafy greens, magnesium is one of the body's busiest minerals, supporting muscles, energy and sleep.
Manganese (Mn)EssentialGrains, tea and some well water. Manganese comes mostly from whole grains, tea and legumes, and the body needs a little of it to build bone and process energy.
Copper (Cu)EssentialPlumbing, shellfish and nuts. Copper reaches us through shellfish, nuts and water that has run through copper plumbing.
Zinc (Zn)EssentialMeat, seeds and supplements. Zinc supports immunity and healing and is found in meat, seeds and many supplements.
Cobalt (Co)EssentialVitamin B12, alloys and pigments. A trace element at the heart of vitamin B12, cobalt also appears in coloured glass, some alloys and certain implants.
Phosphorus (P)EssentialDairy, meat and food additives. Phosphorus pairs with calcium to build bone and teeth and is plentiful in dairy, meat and processed foods with phosphate additives.
Calcium (Ca)EssentialDairy, fortified foods and hard water. Calcium is the mineral most people know, found in dairy, fortified foods and hard water.
ElementTypeWhat it is & where it's found
Lead (Pb)ToxicOld paint, ageing pipes and soil. Often traced to old paint dust, ageing plumbing and disturbed soil, lead is one of the most researched environmental elements.
Cadmium (Cd)ToxicTobacco smoke, some vegetables and batteries. Carried by cigarette smoke and by some leafy crops grown in affected soil, cadmium is an element where the long view tells you most.
Nickel (Ni)ToxicCookware, jewellery, nuts and chocolate. Nickel shows up in more places than you might expect: stainless cookware, jewellery, and foods like nuts and chocolate.
Arsenic (As)ToxicWell water, rice and treated wood. Present naturally in some private wells, in rice, and in older treated timber, arsenic is an element where context really matters.
Barium (Ba)ToxicGroundwater, grains and imaging agents. Barium appears in some groundwater, in foods such as nuts and grains, and in certain imaging products.
Aluminum (Al)ToxicCookware, antiperspirants and antacids. One of the metals we meet most often, aluminum turns up in cookware, antiperspirants and some antacids.
ElementTypeWhat it is & where it's found
Lithium (Li)OtherDrinking water and some medicines. Lithium occurs naturally in some drinking water and is also prescribed to steady mood.
Strontium (Sr)OtherSeafood, toothpaste and mineral water. Strontium follows calcium into bone and is found in seafood, some toothpastes and certain mineral waters.

Vestige is an exposure assessment laboratory test that measures selected elements in a hair sample and returns an informational report comparing those measurements to a reference database. It is meant to help people understand elemental patterns in their hair and learn about common environmental, dietary and lifestyle sources linked to those elements. Results are for educational and informational purposes only. The test does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease or medical condition, and results should not replace advice from a qualified health professional. This test has not been evaluated or approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA).
