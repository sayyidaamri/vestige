Where Does Your Magnesium Come From, and Why Track It?

Written by
Northfield Bio team

Found in nuts, seeds and leafy greens, magnesium is one of the body's busiest minerals, supporting muscles, energy and sleep. Your timeline shows whether your levels held steady across the testing window.

Where does magnesium come from?

Common everyday sources include nuts, seeds and leafy greens. How much reaches you depends on where you live, what you eat and drink, the products you use and the work you do.

Because exposure changes with routines and seasons, a single snapshot can miss the moments that matter. Seeing how levels move over weeks gives far more useful context.

Why measure it over time?

Hair grows about a centimetre a month and keeps a record as it grows. Reading a strand in sequence shows not only how much magnesium is present, but when levels rose or fell, which makes it easier to link changes to everyday life.

Simple ways to lower everyday exposure

  • Know your water. If you use a private well, test it regularly.
  • Vary your diet. Rotating staples spreads out intake from any single source.
  • Check products and cookware. Look for worn coatings and follow care instructions.
  • Ventilate. Fresh air and damp dusting reduce household dust.

This article summarises published research for general education. It is not medical advice. Talk to a qualified clinician about your own health.
